About me

An observational astronomer working on black-hole growth, galaxy evolution, and time-domain astrophysics.

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences (UCAS), working with Professor Xin Wang. I received my PhD in astronomy from Peking University, supervised by Professor Xue-Bing Wu.

My research investigates how black holes grow and how galaxies form and evolve. I develop methods to identify and characterize sources in space-based observations, with particular interests in slitless spectroscopy, active galactic nuclei (AGN), and the populations newly accessible with JWST.

My doctoral work focused on quasar identification in 3D-HST and simulations for the Chinese Space Station Telescope. My recent work extends to the physical nature of little red dots, Hα luminosity functions at cosmic noon, and supernova discovery in multi-epoch JWST imaging.

Methods & collaboration

I work with spectral forward modeling, machine learning for source classification, emission-line and spectral energy distribution fitting, survey-completeness analysis, and multi-epoch imaging. My collaborative projects include quasar catalogs, changing-look AGN, variability, and broad-line-region structure.

Beyond astronomy

Outside research, I enjoy badminton, Go, and reading. At PKU-KIAA, I organized weekly badminton activities in 2020–2021 and represented the School of Physics in the PKU Cup. More recently, I won a championship at the UCAS faculty badminton club.
