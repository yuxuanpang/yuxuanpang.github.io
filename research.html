Recent work · JWST / NIRSpec

The structure and evolution
of little red dots

What powers the unusual emission of little red dots (LRDs)? In a first-author study of 11 LRDs at a characteristic redshift of z ∼ 4, I analyzed medium- and high-resolution NIRSpec spectra to separate broad and narrow Balmer emission.

Broad Hα emission correlates with the optical continuum, supporting a common AGN origin. In contrast, [O III] more closely tracks the UV continuum. These relationships constrain the components behind the LRD spectral energy distribution.

We proposed a clumpy gas envelope extending over tens of light-days to connect the inferred broad-line-region scale and optical luminosity. Black-hole growth estimates and a possible connection to narrow-line Seyfert 1 galaxies remain dependent on the adopted accretion model.

Recent work · GLASS-JWST / NIRISS

Faint galaxies
at cosmic noon

How much star formation occurs in faint galaxies at cosmic noon? I measured Hα luminosity functions at z ∼ 1.3 and z ∼ 2.0, combining JWST/NIRISS spectroscopy with gravitational lensing in Abell 2744.

From 99 selected emitters, the final luminosity-function analysis uses 53 and 29 distinct galaxies in the two redshift bins. Source-by-source completeness and effective-volume estimates account for the spatially varying depth and magnification.

The observations reach line luminosities approximately an order of magnitude below earlier grism surveys. The measured faint-end slopes, −1.50 and −1.60, constrain the abundance of low-luminosity galaxies and their contribution to cosmic star formation.

Recent work · PLATE / JWST NIRCam

Opening the time dimension

Deep fields can also reveal a changing Universe. Through the Pandora cluster Lensing, AGN, and Transient Exploration (PLATE) project, I studied PLATE-23a, a Type II supernova candidate in a galaxy at z = 0.688.

Multi-epoch NIRCam observations from 2022–2025 detect the source in 12 filters. Difference imaging reveals its rise and decline, while Bayesian light-curve classification favors a Type IIP interpretation.

Multiband modeling traces the cooling transient, and host-galaxy analysis places it about 13 kpc from the galaxy center after lensing correction. This first-author study is available as a preprint; the supernova classification is photometric.

PhD research · HST / 3D-HST

Finding quasars
without a color cut

In slitless spectroscopy, an apparently broad emission line may come from high-velocity gas or simply the spatial extent of a galaxy. I developed a selection method using two-dimensional forward modeling to evaluate the evidence for a quasar component.

The method identified 19 quasar candidates at z = 0.12–1.56, including 12 with Chandra detections. Tests recover about 90% of known point-like quasars with detectable Hα or Hβ, with an emission-line-galaxy contamination rate of about 5%.

The broader UV-color range and redder optical slopes of these candidates illustrate how spectroscopy can complement conventional color selection and improve the AGN census.

PhD research · CSST simulations

Preparing for a wide-field
quasar survey

I developed a pipeline connecting mock CSST grism images to spectral extraction, convolutional-neural-network classification, emission-line redshifts, and physical-property estimates.

For mock sources with 18 < mr < 22, classification reaches 99% accuracy. About 90% of successfully classified quasars have precise redshifts, with σNMAD = 0.002. Black-hole mass and Eddington-ratio recovery scatters are 0.13 and 0.15 dex relative to simulation inputs; they exclude virial-estimator systematics.

Under the adopted survey assumptions, the study forecasts approximately 0.9 million new quasars, providing a route from detailed source analysis to large statistical samples.
